# Statutory review brief — Taz4Tech storefront

**For a Lebanese lawyer or consumer-protection adviser.** Everything below is a
question, not a position. The shop's public pages currently state only what the
business itself promises; nothing on them describes what the law requires,
deliberately, because nobody qualified has said yet.

This document is the source of truth in the repository at
`docs/legal-review.md`. Answers should come back as edits to the numbered items;
each one names the exact sentence it would change, so the copy can be updated
without a second round of interpretation.

There are **four pages** in scope — returns, terms, privacy, delivery — plus one
question that is a build rather than a sentence (§5.1).

---

## 1. How the shop actually works

Short, because the answers depend on it.

- **Cash on delivery only.** No card payments, no online payment of any kind, no
  payment taken before the goods are in the customer's hands.
- **No accounts.** No registration, no password, no login. The customer's phone
  number is their identity.
- **Every order is confirmed by telephone** before anything leaves the shop. The
  order is placed on the website; a person then rings the customer.
- **The driver waits while the box is opened.** The customer inspects the goods
  before paying, and can hand them straight back at the door.
- **Nothing is sent to the customer in writing.** No email, no SMS, no printed
  document. There is a confirmation page on the website immediately after
  ordering, at a URL the customer must keep; nothing is delivered to them. See
  §5.1 — this may be the one item that needs software rather than wording.
- **Distance selling, to consumers, inside Lebanon only.** Physical consumer
  electronics: laptops, phones, cables, chargers.
- **Sole trader, not yet commercially registered.** The registration number is
  published on every page as soon as there is one.
- **Not VAT-registered, and whether it must be is a separate open question.**
  Prices are quoted VAT-inclusive.

## 2. Returns — `/returns`

### 2.1 Is a seven-day window lawful, and is it enough?

> "Call us within seven days and we will arrange a replacement or give back what
> you paid."

Seven days is the shop's own choice. **Does Lebanese law set a minimum period,
and is it longer?** If it is, the sentence understates the customer's rights
even though the page also says the promises are not a limit on them.

### 2.2 Is there a right to withdraw without a reason?

The page offers returns when something is **wrong** — damaged, incorrect, or not
what was ordered. It says nothing about changing one's mind.

**Does a distance sale in Lebanon carry a cooling-off or withdrawal right that
applies regardless of fault?** If it does, it is currently unstated, which is
the most significant omission on the page.

### 2.3 Are the exclusions permitted?

> "Anything you damaged after it was delivered, and anything returned without
> the cables, chargers or accessories it came with."

**Can a seller lawfully refuse a return on either ground?** In particular:

- refusing where accessories are missing, when the fault is with the main item;
- the line between customer damage and a latent defect. The page already says a
  fault appearing on its own is not damage, but that is the shop's reading, not
  a legal test.

### 2.4 Who pays for the return, and how is money refunded?

**Neither is stated anywhere, and both will be asked on the first return.**

- Does the customer bear any cost of returning goods, or collecting them?
- Cash was paid at the door. **How must a refund be made, and within what
  period?** Cash in person is the obvious mirror, but "obvious" is not a legal
  answer.

### 2.5 Who chooses — replacement or refund?

> "we will arrange a replacement or give back what you paid"

As written, the shop chooses. **Is that the customer's choice by law**, and does
it depend on the nature of the fault?

### 2.6 Is there a legal guarantee beyond the shop's seven days?

Electronics fail in month three. The page is silent past seven days, which
readers will take as "then it is the manufacturer's problem".

**Does the seller carry a statutory guarantee of conformity, for how long, and
must the page say so?** This is the question most likely to produce a complaint
against the shop.

## 3. Terms of sale — `/terms`

### 3.1 When is the contract formed?

> "An order is a request until we confirm it on the phone."

The page takes a position: the website order is an invitation, and the telephone
call concludes the sale. **Is that enforceable in Lebanon**, or does placing the
order online form the contract regardless of what the terms say?

It matters for §2.2: a withdrawal period, if one exists, would run from a moment
this sentence is trying to define.

### 3.2 Cancelling after the driver has left

> "Call us before the driver leaves and we will cancel the order at no cost to
> you."

Generous before dispatch, silent after. **Is there a right to cancel that
survives dispatch**, and if so must the page say so?

### 3.3 Governing law, jurisdiction, and where to complain

**None of the three appears anywhere on the site.**

- Must the terms state governing law and a competent court?
- Must the shop name a complaints route — the Consumer Protection Directorate at
  the Ministry of Economy and Trade, or otherwise — and give its contact
  details?

### 3.4 What must be disclosed before an order is placed

Law 81/2018 Art. 31 is already relied on for seller identity, which is why the
name, telephone number and (when issued) registration number sit in the footer
of every page.

**Is that list complete for a distance sale?** Specifically, is anything else
required *before* the customer commits — a full geographic address, the total
price including delivery, delivery timescales, the existence and conditions of
any withdrawal right?

Delivery cost is shown at checkout before the order is placed, per governorate.

### 3.5 Changing the terms

> "the version on this page is the one that applies to orders placed after it
> appears"

**Is that sufficient**, or must changes be notified, dated, or archived?

## 4. Privacy — `/privacy`

Included because a lawyer reading three pages can read four, and because Law
81/2018 covers personal data as well as electronic transactions.

### 4.1 Does processing this data need authorisation or notification?

The shop holds a name, telephone number, delivery address and order history, for
delivery and for answering later questions. **Does that require any filing,
licence or authorisation**, and does the answer change once there is a
commercial registration?

### 4.2 Is the disclosure adequate?

The page states what is kept, why, who sees it (the driver), and that it is
neither sold nor used for unsolicited marketing. **Is anything else required** —
a retention period, a named controller, a stated legal basis, a route to
complain about data specifically?

### 4.3 Deletion

> "We keep records of completed orders for accounting, so an order you have
> already received may have to stay on file."

**Is there a statutory retention period for sales records**, and does a customer
have a right to erasure that this sentence should acknowledge?

### 4.4 The cookie claim

> "there is no consent banner because there is nothing to consent to"

One cookie holds the shopping basket. There is no analytics, no advertising and
no third-party script anywhere on the site. **Is a strictly-functional cookie
exempt from any consent requirement in Lebanon?**

## 5. One question that is a build, not a sentence

### 5.1 Must the customer be given something in writing?

**Today they are given nothing.** The confirmation page appears once, at a URL
the customer must keep; no email, SMS or document is sent, and the shop has no
capability to send one.

**If a distance seller must provide written confirmation of the contract — the
order, the price, the terms, any withdrawal right — then this is a feature the
shop does not have**, not a paragraph that needs writing. It would mean adding a
sending channel (email or SMS), which changes what personal data is collected
and brings §4 with it.

Please answer this one first if you answer only one. Everything else is wording.

---

## 6. What has deliberately not been written

So that the absence is not mistaken for an oversight:

- No statement of what any law requires, anywhere on the site.
- No withdrawal or cooling-off period, because whether one exists is §2.2.
- No warranty term beyond the shop's own seven days, because that is §2.6.
- No governing law, jurisdiction or complaints body, because that is §3.3.

What the pages **do** say, and which needs checking rather than writing, is that
the shop's promises do not reduce the customer's legal rights and that where the
two differ the law prevails. That sentence is on both pages, in all three
languages, and is the only thing on the site that refers to law at all.
